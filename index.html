What worked07

Four things you can use this week.

You don’t need to hire anyone for these. They’re a few of the things I did at Vera-Fi Audio that you can try on your own.

Product photos

Shoot every product on the same background, at 1:1.

Shoppers compare products side by side. When every photo matches, the products look like one brand, and the store looks like a store you can trust.

Email

Email the people who already bought from you, by interest.

Past buyers and sign-ups are usually the easiest sales to win. Send each group the emails that match what they bought or asked about. At Vera-Fi Audio, campaigns averaged an 11.6% click rate.

Promotion

Use YouTubers for launches, not for steady sales.

Lead with your award winners the rest of the time. Reviews give a launch its spike, and the store and email have to carry the months in between.

Support

Write the manual so customers don’t have to email you.

A clear, designed manual answers the setup questions before they reach your inbox, and it makes the product feel finished.

Questions11

Will a store take away the personal touch my customers like?

It didn’t at Vera-Fi Audio. Customers still talk to us personally through the store chat and email. Email campaigns are sent by interest, so people get the emails that match what they care about, not a blast to everyone.

You work with an audio brand. Is that a conflict?

I don’t work with direct competitors of my clients. If you make a different kind of equipment, serve a different market, or go for a different sound, it isn’t a conflict. Your numbers stay private unless you agree otherwise.

Because hiring someone you’ve never worked with is a risk. A small fix lets you judge the work, the communication and the timing first. It’s how I started with Vera-Fi Audio: social graphics first, then the store, email and the rest.

Yes. Vera-Fi Audio runs on systems that are already in place, so I have room for small fixes and one larger project at a time.
